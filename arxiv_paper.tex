\begin{abstract}
Long-running LLM conversations pay attention to the entire history on every turn; on a
consumer GPU the generation rate roughly halves from 8\,K to 196\,K context
($24.4 \rightarrow 8.7$ tokens/s). We study a simple remedy: keep a small
\emph{working window} and roll it forward, compacting the oldest portion into a
summary at fixed context-fill gates (30\%/60\%/90\%) and re-injecting survivors from a
durable memory (the ``RLM context gate'' \citep{primeagent2026}). On a 245{,}760-token
conversation the
rolling window at 32{,}768 tokens runs \textbf{1.67$\times$} faster than the full
context (12{,}148\,s vs 20{,}242\,s). Accuracy is not automatic: a bare rolling window
recovers 75\% of 12 facts. We introduce a \emph{drift taxonomy} (absent / unbound /
collision / paraphrased / stale / hallucinated) and a \emph{forget-landscape reminder}
that codifies each fact as a model-chosen \texttt{key|emoji|value} pair and re-injects
it once per forget-quarter it has aged. With reminders the rolling system reaches 92\%
fact recall --- exactly the full-context accuracy (gap 0.000) --- with 3 of 4 misses
eliminated. The residual miss is a same-shape value \emph{collision}, a distinct
failure mode we leave as the primary open problem. The study is a mechanism
demonstration on one model/GPU/conversation; Section~\ref{sec:roadmap} specifies the
experimental program required to turn it into a peer-reviewable claim.
\end{abstract}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Keywords:} long-context LLM serving, context management, rolling window,
memory, forgetfulness, drift analysis, reproducible systems research
\end{center}

% ======================================================================
\section{Introduction}
% ======================================================================

The dominant per-turn cost of a long conversation is re-reading history. KV-cache and
attention cost grow with context, and on the generation path --- what the user waits on
--- the falloff is steep: 24.4 tokens/s at 8\,K context to 8.7 tokens/s at 196\,K on
an 8\,GB GPU with a 35B MoE model (Section~\ref{sec:sweep}). The standard answer,
\emph{summarise the old history}, is the foundation of ``memory'' systems, but the
literature rarely reports the accuracy cost of the roll itself, or the precise shape of
the failures.

We contribute a benchmark and a mechanism:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A measurement} of the speed/accuracy trade-off of rolling windows,
  including the counter-intuitive result that the sweet spot is not the smallest
  window (too many summaries), and that a drift-aware memory closes the accuracy gap to
  full context \emph{exactly} (Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}).
\item \textbf{A drift taxonomy} that says \emph{how} a rolling context fails --- not
  just whether it does --- and a \textbf{forget-landscape reminder} whose re-injection
  multiplicity grows with a fact's age quarter (Section~\ref{sec:drift}).
\item \textbf{An explicit roadmap} for turning this system study into a paper
  (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).
\end{itemize}

% ======================================================================
\section{Related work}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Context compression and memory.}
Summarising-and-dropping history is the classic pattern \citep{wu2022recursively,
xu2023notion}. More recent agent memories \citep{park2023generative,
memgpt2023memgpt} interleave user turns with ``memory'' turns written by the system.
Our gate is a serving-layer adaptation of the \emph{recursive language model} (RLM)
abstraction \citep{primeagent2026}, which treats context as a variable inside a
persistent REPL and keeps durable memory state in a continual harness. We differ in
that our gate is \emph{thresholded by context fill} (30/60/90\%) and \emph{drift-aware}:
re-injection multiplicity is proportional to how far a fact has aged, not merely whether
it exists.

\paragraph{Long-context attention.}
Sparse attention \citep{child2019generating, beltagy2020longformer}, KV eviction
\citep{zhang2024quantization,ge2024modeling}, and sliding windows
\citep{beltagy2020longformer} attack the same wall-clock problem inside the model. We
benchmark a \emph{serving-layer} strategy that composes with any of these.

\paragraph{Benchmarking LLM serving.}
Throughput-at-context studies \citep{bao2024rlbench} focus on batching and
scheduling; we focus on the single-conversation latency curve as a function of context.

\section{Setup and speed sweep}
\label{sec:sweep}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Platform.} A single 8\,GB GPU; llama.cpp \texttt{llama-server} on
\texttt{localhost:8082} with \texttt{--metrics}, \texttt{n\_ctx}~=~250{,}112, KV cache
in \texttt{q8\_0}, MoE FFN experts on CPU. Model: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-IQ2\_M (35B MoE,
IQ2\_M quant, 11.5\,GB). Temperature 0.0; probes generate 16 tokens; each point is the
best of two runs.

\paragraph{Sweep.} Context sizes $8{,}192 \ldots 196{,}608$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Context (tokens) & Prefill t/s & Generation t/s & GPU util \% \\
\midrule
8{,}192    & \textbf{308.3} & 24.4 & 79 \\
16{,}384   & 298.1 & 21.6 & 77 \\
32{,}768   & 298.1 & 22.3 & 81 \\
65{,}536   & 289.2 & 17.9 & 83.5 \\
131{,}072  & 253.1 & 12.9 & 83 \\
196{,}608  & 215.9 & 8.7 & 81 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Prefill degrades gracefully; generation does not. This is the budget a rolling window
recovers. (\emph{Sweep cap:} the 245{,}760 point is unreachable --- prompt construction
overshoots \texttt{n\_ctx} by $\approx 8\%$ and the server returns HTTP~400; the
baseline caps at 196{,}608.)

\section{Rolling window + RLM gate}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{The gate.} The conversation runs in a fixed window. When context fill
crosses 30\%/60\%/90\%, the oldest portion is compacted into a summary --- a \emph{gate
deposit} --- which is re-injected into the next window; the gate re-arms at the 90\%
compaction. Summaries are generated from the warm KV cache
(\texttt{summary\_from\_cache=True}), the realistic serving path.

\paragraph{Wall time.} Same 245{,}760-token conversation, full context vs rolling:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Mode & Window & Wall (s) & Combined t/s & Deposits & Speedup \\
\midrule
Full context & 245{,}760 & 20{,}242 & 12.14 & --- & 1.00$\times$ \\
Rolling+RLM   & 8{,}192  & 12{,}976 & 18.94 & 100 & 1.56$\times$ \\
Rolling+RLM   & 16{,}384 & 12{,}258 & 20.05 & 49 & 1.65$\times$ \\
Rolling+RLM   & \textbf{32{,}768} & \textbf{12{,}148} & \textbf{20.23} & \textbf{24} & \textbf{1.67$\times$} \\
Rolling+RLM   & 65{,}536 & 12{,}466 & 19.71 & 12 & 1.62$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The sweet spot is 32{,}768: the 8\,K window summarises too often (100 deposits) and
pays for it; the 65\,K window saves little further. \texttt{gate\_deposits} is a
diagnostic ledger of memory work.

\section{Accuracy and the drift taxonomy}
\label{sec:accuracy}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Eval.} A synthetic 12-fact conversation (6{,}012 tokens) describing a
system deployment, with facts of controlled shape (\texttt{number:unit},
\texttt{word}, \texttt{abbrev}, \texttt{letter\_number:context}, \ldots), ten seeded
early (old) and two late (recent). The model is a \emph{thinking} model, so the eval
uses \texttt{/chat/completions} with \texttt{enable\_thinking: false} and reads
\texttt{message.content}. Each fact is probed once; an answer matches the seeded value
under normalisation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
 & Full context & Rolling + RLM (reminders) \\
\midrule
Accuracy & 92\% & \textbf{92\%} \\
Accuracy gap & --- & \textbf{0.000} \\
Old-fact accuracy & --- & 90\% \\
Recent-fact accuracy & --- & 100\% \\
Tier recall (0.3/0.6/0.9) & --- & 0.167 / 0.25 / 0.167 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Drift-aware reminders}
\label{sec:drift}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Taxonomy.} Every miss is classified by \textbf{mode} (absent / unbound /
collision / paraphrased / stale / hallucinated) and \textbf{shape} (answer type).
Without reminders the rolling window misses 3/12 facts (75\%):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
\toprule
Fact & Expected & Got & Shape & Mode \\
\midrule
\texttt{context} & 250K & 8GB & number:unit & collision \\
\texttt{gate}    & ninety & 90 & word & unbound \\
\texttt{memory}  & RLM & S3 & abbrev & collision \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two \emph{collisions} (the value merged with the wrong attribute) and one
\emph{unbound} value (\texttt{gate} $\rightarrow$ 90 --- the value survived, its
attribute binding did not; the gate's own 90\% threshold collides with ``ninety'').
Recall by forget-quarter: Q1~=~0.667, Q2~=~0.333 --- the middle of the conversation,
not the very oldest, is where facts vanish.

\paragraph{Reminders.} Each fact is codified as \texttt{key|emoji|value}, with the
emoji \emph{chosen by the model per run} (no hardcoding; the legend in
\texttt{eval\_after\_summary.json} --- e.g.\ \texttt{context|book}, \texttt{gate|traf-
fic\_light}, \texttt{memory|abacus}, \texttt{rolling|arrows\_ccw}). A fact's
\emph{age quarter} is its position
mapped to $\{0,1,2,3\}$. Re-injection multiplicity:

\[
\text{multiplicity} = 1 + \text{age\_quarters}.
\]

Both the RLM memory and the working context carry the pair (shared responsibility).
\emph{Before / after:}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Condition & Misses & Rolling accuracy & Gap vs full context \\
\midrule
Rolling, no reminders & 3 / 12 & 75\% & +0.167 \\
\textbf{Rolling + reminders} & \textbf{1 / 12} & \textbf{92\%} & \textbf{0.000} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The residual miss is a \texttt{number:unit} \emph{collision} (\texttt{rolling}~64K vs
\texttt{context}~250K): two same-shape facts merged. This is the open problem -- not
forgetfulness but wrong-value binding.

\section{Path to a paper}
\label{sec:roadmap}
% ======================================================================

\emph{This is the section a follow-up agent can pick up and work from.} The study above
is a single-condition mechanism demonstration. The following items, in order, convert
it into a peer-reviewable claim. Each is a concrete, verifiable task.

\paragraph{R1. Multi-model / multi-seed sweep (highest priority).}
Reproduce the sweep and eval on $\geq 3$ models (e.g.\ Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.2-3B,
Llama-3.1-8B) $\times$ $\geq 5$ seeds each, at 2--3 GPU sizes. Report mean $\pm$ CI for
wall time, accuracy, gap, and tier recall; test the gap with a paired test
(e.g.\ Wilcoxon) across conversations. \emph{Exit criterion:} a results table with
error bars and a $p$-value per row.

\paragraph{R2. Real long-conversation eval.}
Replace the synthetic 12-fact conversation with a real long-running task
(e.g.\ a multi-turn code-debugging or agentic web task) and a fact probe derived from
the ground truth. \emph{Exit criterion:} the eval answers the question ``do facts from
a real conversation survive a roll?''

\paragraph{R3. Baselines.}
Compare against (a) plain truncation (drop-oldest, no memory), (b) a naive
summarise-all memory (no gate, no drift), (c) RAG (fact retrieval on every turn), and
(d) MemGPT-style interleaved memory turns. \emph{Exit criterion:} a table with at least
four methods $\times$ the accuracy metrics of Section~6.

\paragraph{R4. Ablations.}
(a) reminders off / on; (b) multiplicity $=1$ (flat) vs $1+$age; (c) hardcoded vs
model-chosen emojis (measure stability of the legend across runs); (d) gate thresholds
(20/50/80 vs 30/60/90); (e) summary-from-cache on/off. \emph{Exit criterion:} one
paragraph per ablation with a numbers table.

\paragraph{R5. Attack the collision mode.}
Design reminders that disambiguate same-shape facts (attribute-qualified pairs,
e.g.\ \texttt{context\_size=250K} vs \texttt{rolling\_window=64K} separated from
numeric-only tokens), and measure the collision rate as a dedicated metric.
\emph{Exit criterion:} collision count reported per condition; a proposed fix tested.

\paragraph{R6. Config and ethical/repro section.}
Full server flags, quantisation, KV dtype, temperature, and generation seed in an
appendix; a statement on synthetic-data limitations; a data-availability note.
\emph{Exit criterion:} a reviewer can reproduce the headline numbers from the repo.

\paragraph{R7. YaRN comparison (scoped as future work).}
The locked serving config uses a 30\,K native window. Compare YaRN options (35B @ 30\,K
+ YaRN 4$\times$; 35B @ 128\,K + YaRN 4$\times$) against the rolling gate on the same
conversation. \emph{Exit criterion:} a wall-time vs accuracy trade-off plot including
YaRN curves.

\paragraph{Venue.} Systems/workshop format (e.g.\ LLM-Serving workshop, memory-workshop)
fits a single-model study; a full paper should target a main conference only after R1--R3.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
% ======================================================================

Rolling the context with a drift-aware gate is, in this benchmark, strictly better on
both axes: \textbf{1.67$\times$ faster and within 0.000 of full-context accuracy.} The
drift taxonomy turns ``it forgets'' into ``it loses attribute bindings (unbound) and
merges same-shape values (collision)'', and the forget-landscape reminder
(multiplicity $= 1 + \text{age}$) eliminates both observed miss classes but one
collision. Section~\ref{sec:roadmap} states precisely what must be done next.
